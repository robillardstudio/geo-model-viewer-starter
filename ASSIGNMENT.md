# Training 2 — Found on the Oval

**Concept:** Choose a spot on the Oval and hide a 3D object there. The object stays locked until a participant physically reaches the spot; only then does it open, ready to be placed in AR. Consider how the place itself (a path crossing, a tree, a bench, the Thompson steps) gives meaning to what is found there.

- Choose a spot on the Oval and record its GPS coordinates on site
- Design a 3D mesh (.glb) that responds to that place
- Set your coordinates and radius in the starter, so that reaching the spot opens the .glb
- Test on site, place the object in AR, and document it (screen record)

**Delivery**

> Communicate your intent in class
> Demo on the Oval in class
> Push your project to a GitHub repo (not GitHub Pages) and submit the repo link here. The README must include your coordinates, a one-line intent, and the screen-recorded video (or a link to it).

Your video must show both moments: walking to the spot until the object opens, then the object placed in AR.

Repo link due Oct 26, 12pm, or send to robillard.11@osu.edu.

Additionally bring your phone or iPad (location services on).

**Exercise Tips**

- Test at your desk first with `?fake`, then on site
- GPS drifts 5–15 m: use a radius of 15–25 m and pick a spot in the open, away from buildings
- Use simple geometries (no 3D scan); keep the .glb under 5 MB, 1 unit = 1 m
- Think about what it means to find something only by being there

**Rubric (5 points)**

**Response to place (2):** Does the spot matter? Is the relation between the site and the object legible and felt on location?

**Precision in location and scale (2):** Does the object open reliably at the intended spot? Is its scale in AR accurate and meaningful in the space?

**Work Ethics (1):** Is the repo delivered on time with a clear README? Is the communication clear?
